## The map

The force field is 11 km across, from Lansdown and the racecourse down to Wellow, and from Newton
Park to Bathford, with Combe Down in the middle, and a strip of it reaches out further north, up
Bath Road over Lansdown to Sir Bevil Grenville's monument. The city is built to the same detail as the
village (gardens, walls, doors, lamps, parked cars, shop signs) in squares about a kilometre across
that load in around you, and it all plays the same everywhere: traffic, naughty boys, cats,
pickups, buses, cars and balls on the pitches, in town just as in the village.

Up on Bathampton Down stands Eric's oak, in memory of our friend Eric Benjamin Holmes. Down in
Twerton is Twerton Park, Bath City's ground: the old Main Stand with BATH CITY picked out in its
seats, the covered Popular Side, the big open Bristol End, the shallow Bath End, the dugouts,
hoardings and floodlights. You can climb the terraces and sit in the stand.

### Landmarks

Bath's great buildings are modelled by hand:

- **Bath Abbey**: the ladders of angels up the west front's turrets, the great windows, flying
  buttresses and pinnacles, and the tower. Go in through the west door to the nave under its fan
  vault, with the stained glass, pews, choir and organ, or [climb it](#climbing-bath-abbey).
- **Pulteney Bridge**: three arches over the Avon, Adam's front with its Venetian window and domed
  pavilions, the odd backs of the shops upstream, and the names of the shops there now. Pulteney
  Weir's horseshoe of white water is just below.
- **The Roman Baths**: in from the Abbey Churchyard through the Concert Room, under its glass dome,
  onto the terrace with its emperors, and down the stairs to the steaming green Great Bath (or
  jump in). The Pump Room's columned front, with "water is best" in Greek along its frieze, faces
  the Abbey.
- **Great Pulteney Street**: Baldwin's long Georgian street from Laura Place, both sides one
  design, block by block between the side streets.
- **The Holburne Museum** at its far end: the three-arched loggia you can walk up into, its
  portico and urns, and the glass extension in Sydney Gardens behind.
- **Argyle Street**, from the bridge to Laura Place, a shopfront to every house.
- **Grand Parade** along the river: Davis's colonnade and river wall, with a balustrade, urns and
  cast-iron lamps that light up after dark.
- **The Empire Hotel** on Orange Grove: its domed octagonal tower, and the roofline of a castle, a
  house and a cottage.
- **Sham Castle** on Bathampton Down: Ralph Allen's folly, all front, with an arch you can walk
  through.
- **Prior Park** above Combe Down: Allen's Palladian mansion, its giant portico looking down the
  valley, the steps up from the lawn, and its wings stepping down the hill.
- **Kingswood School** on Lansdown: Wilson's Tudor Gothic range with its tall entrance tower, and
  the chapel below.
- **The Bell Inn** on Walcot Street, the co-op pub: its red pub front under an 18th-century ashlar
  house, the rusticated doorway, the bell hanging over the pavement, and the old stables beside it
  with their faded painted sign. Walk in: the long bar with its hand pumps, settles, wood burner,
  piano and the little stage where the bands play, posters for Awful Sparks gigs on every wall; the games room with
  bar billiards, table football, pool and the launderette; then up the stairs at the back (or up
  the yard through the gates) to the garden, with the Pizza Bike, picnic tables under strings of
  lights, covered seating and the Back Bar with its own stage.
- **Sir Bevil Grenville's monument**, out on Lansdown where the battle was fought in 1643: a stone
  pedestal on steps with the Grenvilles' griffin on top.

### Streets, walls and bridges

Roads, paths, the canal and the railways cross on proper bridges, with Bath-stone parapets,
abutments, piers and arches, and whatever they cross running on underneath (the Dundas Aqueduct
carries the canal over the Avon and the railway). Roads rise and fall like roads, in cuttings and
on embankments, and river banks slope down to the water.

Walls the lidar sees stand at their measured height, and streets are as wide as the walls either
side allow, with a pavement a kerb high only where there is one. Every garden has its own front
wall, gate and path to the door, with one fence or hedge between it and each neighbour. Jump at a
wall up to 2.6 m high and you pull yourself up onto it.

### Day and night

Day turns to night and back every 24 minutes, the same for everyone since it comes from the clock.
At night the stars and moon come out, street lamps light the pavements, windows light up across
Bath, and traffic puts its headlights on (they come on in tunnels too). On a computer you can see
about 900 m by day and the lamps' glow from right across town at night; phones and iPads see a
little over half as far, to keep them smooth.

The watch on your wrist, bottom left above your health, tells the game's time: a black G-Shock
with the hours and minutes, the day of the week and the date (a new game day every 24 minutes).
After dark its backlight glows blue-green so you can still read it.

## Menus, settings and the debug panel

**Full screen** is in the pause menu (Esc opens it and Esc again closes it, or II on a touch screen). In Chrome and Edge, Esc then
only pauses and you hold Esc to leave full screen; it stays full screen down the mines and in the
deli too. Safari and Firefox leave full screen when you press Esc.

**Settings**, on the main menu and in the pause menu, is remembered on that computer:

- Picture: draw distance (600 m to 6 km; squares more than about 850 m off are drawn simpler),
  resolution (Lowest, about 270 lines, through Normal, about 540, to Full; fewer if it runs slowly
  on a big or Retina screen), field of view (60 to 100 degrees), shadows, the retro look and the
  debug panel.
- Controls: mouse sensitivity.
- Play: passive mode (also a tickbox on the main menu), where cats, naughty boys, the teams, the
  Flag Lads, Bendy and seagull muck all leave you alone, even if you shoot them (other players can
  still shoot you, and traffic and trains can still run you over). The host's setting is the game's: everyone who
  joins plays by it, and if the host changes it mid-game it changes for everyone.
- Sound: music, sound effects, background (the traffic, birds and wind round you) and voice chat volumes.
- Reset to defaults puts them all back.

**The debug panel** (F3, or Debug info in the pause menu on a phone or iPad) shows where you are
(game coordinates, latitude and longitude, OS grid reference, which way you face and which square
you're in), the frame rate and draw calls, whether the sound is running (and any sound that went
wrong and was cut off so the rest could still be heard), and how your link to each player is doing,
with their version beside their name. Shift+F3 or its Copy button copies your position, and O or Go to takes
east,north (or a copied link) and puts you there. With it open, T moves the clock on three hours
for everyone in your game, and L steps the draw distance through 600 to 6000 m.

## Finding your way

**The minimap and the big map** (M, or tap the minimap) show the ground at the same detail all
over Bath. The big map zooms with the wheel, + and −, or a pinch, and drags about. Its key says
what everything is; click a row to hide that kind of thing on all the maps, and again to bring it
back. Other players show as arrows in their colour, with a red ring while they're shooting; on the
minimap anyone out of range sits on the rim in their direction. Doors into the minigames are a
white star in a cyan ring, and the cable car is a red and white line.

**Markers**: click or tap a spot on the big map, or press G while looking at one, to put down your
marker in your colour. Everyone sees it on both maps, as a ring of light in the street and as a pin
on the compass strip with how far away it is. Do it again to move it, tap it again to take it down,
and it goes when you leave.

**Go to a pub**, in the pause menu, is a map of the whole force field with a pint on every pub in
it, about a hundred, drawn like the big map, with everyone's map markers and the other players on it. Point at one for its name, distance and food hygiene rating, and click it to
be on the pavement outside, facing the door. You can't go while dead or within five seconds of
being hurt, and once you've been to a pub, that's where you come back to life.

## Getting about

### Rides

Rides turn up round the streets, pink on the minimap: a racing bike, a BMX, rollerblades, a
skateboard, a scooter, a pogo stick, and shopping trolleys outside the shops. Walk over one to have
it and press X (Ride) to get on or off.

A and D steer, W and S speed up and slow down, and the hills are the whole game: downhill the wind
roars and the view widens, uphill it bleeds away. Space (Jump) does each ride's move: the racing
bike tucks, the BMX bunny hops, the skateboard ollies, the rollerblades jump, the scooter kicks for
a burst, the pogo stick bounces (time it on landing for a perfect one) and on a trolley you hop on
the back and hold on. Space in the air does a trick.

Come down near a kerb, wall, railing or parked car and you grind it; Shift (Trick) grinds one
you're alongside, or holds a manual on the flat. Airs, spins, tricks, grinds and manuals chain into
a combo, banked when you roll on the ground for a moment, and your bests on each ride are kept.
Come down sideways or mid-trick and you bail, back on in under two seconds. Someone pushing a
trolley can give a mate a lift: they walk up to the basket and press E to climb in.

Speed traps (yellow boxes on poles, on the maps as yellow cameras) at the foot of Bath's steepest
streets clock you as you ride through, and a manual down Milsom Street is timed. Both keep your
best.

### Ladders

Ladders turn up round the streets (white on the minimap), and a ladder lad always leaves his behind
when you put him down. Walk over one to carry it, face a wall and press X (Put up) and it runs up
to the roof's edge, up to 15 m. Anyone can climb it: walk into its foot and W goes up. To come
down, walk off the roof's edge over the top of it, and Space lets go. E at the foot, or on the roof
over its top, takes it down again.

You carry one thing at a time, a ride or a ladder, shown over your rounds bottom right. X uses it;
with your hands full, E swaps what you've got for what you're standing on.

### Cars and buses

The cars parked along The Firs and Fox Hill have their keys in: Ford Escort XR3is, Reliant Robins,
Vauxhall Novas, Austin Metros, Ford Sierras, a Police Sierra (siren and lights), a Transit, a Mr
Whippy van (Greensleeves), a milk float and a Sinclair C5. Badgerline buses wait at stops, and at
any stop you can press E, then 1 for a minibus or 2 for a double-decker, and a driver brings one to
you. Each cricket club has its heavy roller out. Arleen's coaches, from Peasedown St John, are about too, in white with
ARLEEN in blue and red along the sides and the three circles: Plaxton Paramounts and Van Hool
Alizées from the 80s and 90s, a couple waiting at the bus station and one parked up by each of the
sights for the tourists (the Royal Crescent, the Circus, the Abbey, Charlotte Street), and now and
then one in the traffic on the main roads, far too heavy to shove about. You can drive them off, and their passengers get the best view in Bath from the high seats. The XR3i is the hot one: air dam, skirts, pepperpot
alloys and a massive wing on the back, in Diamond White, Radiant Red and the rest, and they're
everywhere: about one car in eight in the traffic round Bath is an XR3i, and so is about one in ten
of the parked cars, and every one of them drives like the real thing. Every other car about, parked or in the traffic, is drawn as
its make: a little hatchback for a Fiesta, Corsa, 206 or Clio, a longer one for a Focus or Astra,
and a tall one-box for a Micra, Yaris or Jazz. Every Golf is a Mk1 GTI (round lamps in a black grille
with a red stripe round it, black arches, slotted alloys, in Mars Red, Diamond Silver and the like),
and it goes very nearly as well as the XR3i. Their paint and windows catch the sky, so even
a black car shows up in the shade. Out in the country, where the houses thin out, a lot of the parked
cars are old Land Rovers in their classic colours (Bronze Green, Pastel Green, Limestone, Marine
Blue, Sand, Poppy Red and the rest), and you'll see the odd one in town and in the traffic. A Land
Rover pulls away smartly and keeps up with anything on the road, fields, verges and tracks are the
same to it as the road, and it takes the hills better than anything else.

Press E (Get in) to take the wheel, or a passenger seat where you can look about and shoot (the
top deck is the place to be). W and S drive and brake, A and D steer, Space is the handbrake (with
some lock on, a donut), H, the up arrow or a click is the horn, siren or chimes, and E gets out. Slow down for a corner and it turns as
tight as a real car does; take one too fast and the front tyres squeal and run wide, scrubbing off
speed, until you lift off or brake and the nose tucks in. The left and right arrows (◀ and ▶ on a
touch screen) work the indicators, which tick away and cancel themselves once you're round the
corner; the brake lights come on as you slow, and the reversing lights as you back up, and everyone
sees them, the traffic's brake lights too. C (View on a touch screen) switches
between the chase camera and the driver's seat, looking out over the dash: the speedo shows your
speed, the wheel turns as you steer, and the buses have a cab of their own, with a big flat wheel and
an old ticket machine by the door. It remembers which you like. Each engine has a voice of its own:
it turns over on the starter and catches when you get in, a diesel clatters and chugs at tickover,
the note dips as you change up, and a petrol engine pops and crackles when you lift off at high revs.
The tyres roar on the road and squeal in a donut, and anything going past drops in pitch as it goes by. Anyone you hit
at speed goes flying (a naughty boy picks himself up and runs off); the traffic and parked cars get knocked spinning out of the way. T-bone a car
hard and it rolls, and one left on its roof catches fire and goes up, hurting anything nearby but
never players or cats. The fireball flashes orange over the street round it, which you see best at
night.

#### Driving the bus

Take the wheel of a bus or minibus and you're on a route: the 2 (the bus station, Widcombe, Prior
Park Road and Ralph Allen Drive, up to Combe Down) or the old 4 (the bus station, through town and up
Lansdown Road to Lansdown), whichever runs nearer, there and back and round again. The route is a
green line on the minimap and the big map, with its stops along it and the next one ringed in
yellow; every bus stop round about is on the minimap anyway, as a little white disc. A card over the
speedo says where you're heading, the next stop and how far, and who's on.

People wait at the stops: pull in by one and stop, and they get on, each paying their fare at the
ticket machine. When someone's stop is next they ring the bell (ding ding, and BUS STOPPING lights
up), and you have to stop at the next stop: they get off with a "Cheers drive". Go straight past and
they complain from the back until you next stop the bus, anywhere at all, and then they get off and
tell you what they think of you (numpty is the kindest of it). Drive past people waiting and you
leave them standing. Get out and the shift's paused: back in the same bus within two minutes and it
carries on. The passengers are yours alone: anyone riding along sees the bus stop and go, but not who
gets on.

On Bathwick Street, outside No. 23 Chinese Kitchen, a BMW X5 has broken down, with a recovery
truck in front and its owner pacing the pavement, telling anyone who'll listen. He can't be hurt:
shoot him and he complains about that too.

### The canal

The Kennet and Avon Canal runs round the east of the map, from the Avon at Widcombe up the six
locks of the Widcombe flight, through Sydney Gardens' tunnels, past Bathampton and Claverton and
over the Dundas Aqueduct, with the Somerset Coal Canal's arm to Brassknocker Basin. The locks work:
press E (Use) at a balance beam and the gates swing, the water roars and the chamber fills or
empties. Narrowboats chug along and wait at each lock. Hop aboard to ride on the roof, or press E
at the stern to take the tiller: W and S set the throttle, A and D put the tiller over. It answers
slowly, so steer early and straighten early.

### Trains and the cable car

InterCity 125s run on the Great Western main line through Bath Spa, Oldfield Park and the Twerton
tunnels, and Sprinters down the Limpley Stoke valley line, stopping at the stations. They keep to
the clock, so everyone sees the same ones; they sound their horns at anyone on the line, then run
them down. Get up on the roof of one and it takes you with it.

Bath Spa is Brunel's station as it stands: go in through the front door on the forecourt off
Dorchester Street and the stairs take you straight up through the building to platform 2, with the
booking hall beside them. Platform 1, across the tracks, has its own stairs up from the south side
by the car park (go round under the railway by Railway Place). Both platforms have their canopies on
columns, benches, the green "Bath Spa" boards, and a departure board: the next trains, where each is
bound and where it calls, which platform, and how many minutes till it's due. Stand on any platform
and the middle of the screen tells you the next train from it too.

You can get on a train at a station. Stand on a platform while its doors are open and press E: a
train that goes on to another town offers it. The train to **Bradford-on-Avon** leaves from Bath
Spa's platform 2, heading east (the board shows it bound for Westbury, calling at Freshford,
Avoncliff and Bradford-on-Avon); it rides the valley line and sets you down on the platform there. The
screen goes black for the journey, and it's the same game: same room, same scoreboard. You step off
with the train you rode standing at the platform behind you, doors open, ready to take you on or
back. Take a train back, or catch a later one with your friends. Bradford-on-Avon is a town of its
own on the map, built from the same data as Bath.

The cable car Curo proposed in 2017 runs from Mulberry Park down to the river at Bath Spa, out over
Lyncombe Vale to a tower on Beechen Cliff. Walk into a cabin while its doors are open and it takes
you across, or stand on its roof for the view.

### Hot air balloons

Early every morning five hot air balloons are filled on the lawn in Royal Victoria Park, west of
Marlborough Buildings: laid out from before dawn, filling from a quarter to five, and standing ready
from half past five till half past ten (game time, so about five real minutes in every 24), when
the ones left are packed away. A few more go up on their own flights soon after dawn and drift off
over the city. Walk up to a basket and press E to climb in. The first one in flies it, and up to
three friends can ride along.

There's no steering: you go where the wind takes you. **W** fires the burner and heats the air in
the envelope, **S** opens the vent at the top to let it out, and **A** and **D** turn the basket
(on a touch screen, push the stick up to burn, down to vent and sideways to turn). The heat takes a
few seconds to tell, so burn in short blasts and wait: let it cool and you sink. The wind blows
harder higher up and comes from a different way, so choosing your height is how you choose where
you go. The panel shows how high you are over the ground, how fast you're climbing or sinking, and
how hot the envelope is. You can't climb out till you're down again (anywhere: a field, a street, a
roof); come down too hard and it hurts. A balloon left on its own comes down and is packed up where
it lands, and the crews have it back on the lawn before the next dawn. `?time=06:00` in the address
stops the clock in the morning, to try it.

## Jobs

Every game day a fresh set of jobs goes up on noticeboards at real places round Bath, a few near
the village and the rest across town, each a badge on the maps. Walk up to one and press E (Use) to
take it on. Everyone in the game is on the same job at once, with a card at the top left saying how
it's going, and the reward drops where it finishes. Everyone gets the same noticeboards that day,
and a job you've done stays ticked off till the next. Call off a job from the pause menu.

**Today's jobs**: when you join, the front page of the Bath Chronicle shows three little missions
for the day (shoot some flags down, win a pub back, ride a couple of kilometres, land a combo, have
a pint...) and, as the lead story, how the Flag Lads are doing. Hold Tab to see it again. They're the same three for everyone, new each midnight.

### The Flag Lads

The Flag Lads are out to cable-tie an England flag to every lamp post in Bath, whether Bath likes
it or not. They're made up, and the joke's on them (the flag-planting, the "taking our streets
back" from Swindon, mostly), not on the flag itself.

Where they're in charge the lamps fly the flag, with bunting once a square's mostly taken, and a
meter under the minimap shows how much of Bath is flagged and how many are down in your square. A
square flies 30 flags at most; shoot every one and it stays clear unless the squares next door fill
up. You can hear the nearest one flapping once you're close. Ladder lads in hi-vis come to put them
back up and run rather than fight; catch one and the ladders stop round there for a while.

Every few minutes the Flag Lads take over a pub. Get to the purple marker before the clock runs out
and clear them out, the Gaffer last, with bonus prizes inside 90 seconds. The crew grows with the
number of you, and with five or more they take two pubs at once. Each Gaffer has a silly name and a
trait or two (a gazebo that soaks up shots, a megaphone, a right-hand man, the whole WhatsApp
group), and he remembers: win a pub off you and he comes back tougher. A pub won back flies an ALL
WELCOME banner, leaves a ride outside, puts out free rounds and keeps the lads out of that square
for the day. Get the meter to 0% and Bath is flag-free till tomorrow.

### Wanted

A ringleader with a silly title ("the Duke of Disorder") hides out with his crew. The poster says
the one loud thing he wears, his routine (a pub, a park, a bus stop, round and round) and two
quirks: slippery, lookouts or mob-handed. A hidden third is given away by the first clue. A search
circle 300 m across goes on the map, and witnesses and dropped leaflets (E) shrink it. Spot him up
close, or through binoculars or a scope, and everyone gets a marker on him. Put him down for the
prizes, or wear him down until he gives up, cuff him (E) and walk him to the police at the nearest
bus stop or police box for more, while his mates try to spring him. Each bounty is six to eight
minutes, with one second chance if he slips the net.

### Treasure trails

A chain of four to six riddles, each about a real pub, church, park or street in Bath ("Bells ring
for Saint Mary"), with a biscuit tin on a post nearby holding the next one. The riddles get harder
with the stars, and now and then one is a sepia sketch of the view instead. Each leg comes with its
own ride. Within 400 m a search circle goes on the map and shrinks as you close in; stuck for two
minutes, E gives a hint at a cost. The last tin is the Bath Bun chest, buried in a mound: dig it up
for a gold, silver or bronze rosette pinned on your jacket. Some trails you do together (with twin
tins that must be opened within 30 seconds of each other), some are races, and one a day is the
trail of the day, the same in every room.

### Races

From the noticeboard to a real place 1 to 3 km away, on a ride that suits the land in between.
Everyone starts on the line together after a countdown, then it's any way you like: finding the
shortcuts is the skill. Splits flash green or red against your best, there's gold, silver, bronze
and a hidden author's time, and you race ghosts of your best run and the room's fastest. Backspace
(Again) puts just you back on the line. There's a daily race round the village too, with a board
of times and the local legend.

### Seagull Attack

Tourists on benches with sandwiches, and the gulls of Bath want them. Five waves come in off the
roofs: herring gulls that swoop (shoot them), hoverers that drop straight down (stare them out with
your crosshair), gangs of black-headed gulls (shout at them with E), walkers that sneak up behind,
and poopers that splatter your screen. The last wave brings Big Lad, a seagull the size of a bus,
who drops poo bombs and dives for whole sandwiches; his beak's the weak spot. Between waves, pick a
crate (chips as a decoy, a plastic owl, a brolly, a megaphone, second helpings). Stars for how much
sandwich is left, a daily twist (bank holiday, Chip Tuesday, a hen do, bin day), and Backspace for
another go.

## Always there

### Climbing Bath Abbey

No clock, just a hard way up for those who find it. One of the ladders of angels on the west
front's turrets can really be climbed (step onto the stone at its foot), all the way to the crown.
Then over the roofs and along the nave's ridge to the tower, up onto the ledge round it, a running
jump over the gap 27 m up, and up the stones jutting from the tower, round all three faces and over
the battlements. Fall and you land on whatever's below.

On top, a pair of angel's wings floats over the roof (and the first time, a halo for your
wardrobe). Walk into them and jump off: you glide, glowing gold, Space flaps you higher and looking
down dives, for 45 seconds. The wings come with a heavenly guitar: look up for the high notes, down
for the low ones, turn to shred, and everyone nearby hears your solo.

### Ball games

Pitches are marked out for their sport: football goals, rugby posts and cricket wickets. Now and
then a ball turns up on one (a white dot on the map): a penalty or a shot from the edge of the box,
a kick at goal from the tee, or a ball to bowl at the stumps. Walk up to it, A and D aim, W and S
set the power, Space or a click kicks or bowls, and E walks off. Each ball has its own wind to
allow for. Score and a prize lands: Bucks Fizz, a sniper rifle, rounds, or Lucozade, which doubles
your damage for 30 seconds. Then the telly takes over, with replays in letterbox and slow motion.

### Get Wood

Kingswood's bonfire, up on Lansdown. The tarmac court on the north side of Fonthill Road, just
along from College Road (a badge on the maps), has the woods climbing the hill behind it, and
bundles of firewood lie about in there: some by the path that runs up through the trees, the rest
scattered among them. E picks one up; carry it down to the court and walk up to the pile and it
goes on. It takes 25 bundles to build the bonfire, and everyone in the game builds the same one.

There's no clock to beat: the day just goes by. Once the bonfire's built, the next time night falls
(straight away, if it's finished after dark) a crowd in lab coats walks over from Westwood with
sparklers. The first one there lights the bonfire, and once they're all round it they take turns to
bend down and light rockets, all night: you can see and hear them from across town, and each burst
lights up the trees below. (F3's debug panel has a Bonfire night button, N, that builds it and
moves the clock on to dark.) By morning it's burnt out, leaving a ring of ash, and a new pile is
started. Not finished by dark? Nothing's lost: what's on the pile stays there for tomorrow, even if
you leave and come back another time.

While there's wood to fetch, a pair of prefects in suits turns up behind anyone who comes near the
court and follows them about, telling them to hurry up, do more, and stop dawdling. They don't carry
any wood themselves.

### The wardrobe

Daft things to wear, earned round Bath and seen by everyone: a seagull on your head, a splattered
flat cap, a traffic cone for winning a race, a centurion's helmet for a gold trail, a halo for
climbing the Abbey, a deerstalker for bringing in a ringleader, a chef's hat from the deli, a cat
on your shoulder for surviving a swarm, a baby's bonnet for ten minutes of tummy time, and more.
One thing on your head, one on your face and one on your back, picked in the Wardrobe in the pause
menu. What you've earned is kept in this browser.

## Fighting and trouble

### Weapons and pickups

You start with a rifle and 120 spare rounds. 1, 2 and 3 pick your fists, the rifle and the sniper
rifle, and right click aims. The sniper rifle has 10-round magazines, a bolt and a scope that
sways. Pints (40 health) wait outside the nearest pubs, and sniper rifles, rounds and the odd pint
turn up outside shops and pubs near you. The middle of Firs Field fills with either a sniper rifle
or a bottle of Co-op Bucks Fizz, which fills you right up. Run dry and it's fists.

It's paintball by default: the guns are markers with a hopper of loose balls on top that roll and
rattle as you move and shoot. Each player's paint is their own colour and splats where it lands.
For real guns and blood, add `?gore=1` to the address (`?gore=0` to go back) or type `claret`
while playing. The browser remembers it, and it's only what you see.

Press Z (or tap Crouch twice) to lie on your front: you crawl, harder to hit, but to everyone else
you're a baby doing tummy time on a play mat. F (Torch) switches your torch on, and everyone sees
the beam.

### Naughty boys

Gangs hang about in alleys, at bus stops and on street corners all over Bath, Fox Hill most of all,
and some arrive in a Nova. Get spotted and they chase you: faster than a walk, slower than a
sprint, and they give up if you get away or out of sight. If they catch you they hit you.

Nobody dies. Splat one enough and he goes over, then picks himself up and legs it, far faster than
you can run, shouting something on his way, and fades away once he's well off: he's had enough,
that's all. The same goes for the teams and the Flag Lads. Hit one with a car and he's sent
sprawling to the side, then gets up and runs off square to the way you were going.

The rugby and cricket teams turn out at their own pitches: one team when you walk on to a rugby
pitch or cricket field (and another when a friend joins you there), then another every three to
five minutes while anyone's still on it. They're bigger and quicker than naughty boys, and they
come looking for anyone near their pitch.

### Cats

Cats keep to the residential streets: big and small, the odd kitten or fat one, long-haired ones of
most breeds. Shoot one and the nearest come for you, with more trickling in. They're faster than
you, but get 80 m from where it started and they give up, and you get 30 seconds' grace after.
After dark they're braver: more are out, the calm ones sit round your feet, and more come for you.
Toot a car horn at them and the calm ones near it bolt, flat out away from you, for a few seconds
(a swarm that's after you takes no notice).

### Bendy

Up at the top of town, within 100 m of 1 Fonthill Road, lives Bendy: four metres of black rubbery
limbs with a pale grinning face and a lamp in his forehead. He only comes out at night, sweeping
his patch with his lamp like a lighthouse. Get caught in the beam and it goes red and locks on, and
he sinks into the ground and pops up beside you, again and again, so running doesn't help. He takes
over a hundred rounds (under fifty in the head), so bring friends; a roof is out of his reach. He
melts into a puddle of ink and leaves three of the best prizes, and a few minutes later he's back.

By day Bendy has another life, at an ordinary size: a history teacher on a school trip, at
Grenville's monument at the far end of the force field's strip up Bath Road (look for the Kingswood
School minibus parked on the lane, which you can drive). Go up to him and he tells you about the
Battle of Lansdown, a fact at a time. At dusk he sinks into the ground, and that's the lesson over.
Shooting him gets you nothing but a telling-off.

Just over the way, Jamie Mair has a crafty smoke on Lansdown: some of the time at the top of Van
Diemen's Lane, by Lansdown Road, and the rest in the bushes between Kingswood School's chapel and
Lansdown Road. Walk right up to him and he gives you one. While you smoke it, for a minute, nothing
can hurt you and every hit you land does double (you glow red, and the edges of your view throb).
He'll give you another a minute after it's out. Shooting him does nothing but annoy him.

## Minigames

The doors into them are points of interest on the maps, with a neon sign and a ring of light on the
step.

**Under Combe Down** is a co-op dungeon crawl in the stone mines under the village, through the
front door of 1 The Firs or from the front page (`web/mines.html`). Pick a class, go down with your
party and fight monsters, mini-bosses and bosses in the dark. Every player generates the same level
from a shared seed.

**COMBE DOWN WITH ME** is a co-op cooking game in the kitchen of the Darling Deli, for one to four
cooks (`web/deli.html`). Go in under the orange neon sign, or pick it from the front page. You all
work one three-minute shift: fetch from the crates, chop, fry and bake, plate up and hand it
through the pass. The menu is a Bath Bun, a Bacon Butty, a Ploughman's, a Cheese & Tomato Sarnie
and The Full Combe Down. Anything left on the heat burns, quick service tips better, and takings
earn up to three stars. WASD walk, Space or E picks up and puts down, and holding F or Shift chops.
Your mic is open in the kitchen.

## Playing together

Multiplayer is peer to peer over WebRTC: type a game name (like `HADLEY`) and press Play. If that
game is running you join it, on its map; if not, you start it. Tell your friends the name or share
the link. There is no game server. If everyone has gone down the mines, it offers to take you after
them.

Joining a running game, you wait on the menu until you've linked up with everyone ("Connecting to 2
of 3 players…"), or 15 seconds. If a link drops, the two of you find each other again within a few
seconds. A notice pops up when someone joins or leaves, and the league table (hold Tab) shows the
game's name, map and players.

Everyone in a game plays the same version. A page left open since the game last changed updates
itself when you press Play. Joining a newer game, you're asked to refresh; joining an older one,
you're told to wait while they do, and they get a banner saying so.

**Voice chat**: your mic is open to players nearby, heard in 3D and fading out by 60 m. Hold V
(Radio) to talk to the whole room through a crackly walkie-talkie. The browser asks for the mic
when you press Play, and you can mute it from the pause menu.

Other players wear a jacket in their own colour, with their own face and hair. Their heads turn to
look where they're looking, their legs walk, run and crouch, and their mouths move as they talk.

## Look and sound

A modern take on 90s Build-engine shooters: chunky pixel-art textures drawn in the browser, leafy
swaying trees that become flat pictures facing you further off, Duke Nukem style, tufts of grass
with buttercups and daisies, and a punchy colour grade with a fine dither, drawn at about 540 lines
and scaled up in crisp square pixels. The sound effects and the soundtrack (procedural 90s-shooter
metal) are synthesised in the browser, so the game ships no audio files. Under it all is the sound of
where you are, anywhere in Bath: a far-off murmur of traffic in town, birdsong in the woods and gardens
(sparrows, a blackbird, wood pigeons, crows), the wind gusting across open ground and up on the roofs,
a skylark over the fields, and by night a quieter town and the odd owl. It goes muffled indoors and in a car.
